# How Do You Feel? Emotions & Emojis — Script (≈20 min of slides, 45 min with the printed tasks)

Open http://localhost:5173/emotions (or pick it on the home page). `[CLICK]` = press the clicker once. There is no sound: you do the talking.

The emojis are alive: they fall in, blink, move the way each emotion feels, look at each other and bump into their neighbours. They follow the mouse pointer, and **clicking an emoji makes it jump** without changing the slide (click on the paper, or use the clicker, to move on). Try letting a student click one.

**The activity is online, nothing to print:** students open **public-speaking-drab.vercel.app/feel** on their phones (slide 16 shows the link and a QR code), about 4 minutes. They match emojis and words, fill in sentences, tap the right emoji and climb a ladder, then draw one emoji with a finger. The last screen shows their name, score, drawing and sentence: nothing is sent to you, so ask them to show it. If you can print one day, the same exercises are in `public/emotions/` as PDFs (`/emotions/print` in the browser), with card games and a page of answers.

1. *(blank screen)* `[CLICK]` An emoji falls in. Ask: **How do you feel today?** Take two or three answers.
2. `[CLICK]` **Emoji is a Japanese word.** `[CLICK]` *e* means picture. `[CLICK]` *moji* means character. A picture character.
3. `[CLICK]` **Feeling good.** The emojis fall in. Ask the class to guess each emotion before you show the word (each one jumps when its word appears): `[CLICK]` happy, `[CLICK]` delighted, `[CLICK]` excited, `[CLICK]` relaxed.
4. `[CLICK]` **Feeling down.** Same game: `[CLICK]` sad, `[CLICK]` disappointed, `[CLICK]` bored, `[CLICK]` tired.
5. `[CLICK]` **Uh-oh!** `[CLICK]` worried, `[CLICK]` scared, `[CLICK]` terrified, `[CLICK]` embarrassed.
6. `[CLICK]` **Grrr… or wow!** `[CLICK]` annoyed, `[CLICK]` angry, `[CLICK]` furious, `[CLICK]` surprised.
7. `[CLICK]` **-ed or -ing?** I am bored: that is how I feel. `[CLICK]` This class is boring: that is what makes me feel it. *(Smile: they will enjoy that one.)* `[CLICK]` -ed is how you feel, -ing is what makes you feel it.
8. `[CLICK]` **Who is happier?** Let them answer first. `[CLICK]` Ben is happier than Ana. We use a comparative and *than*.
9. `[CLICK]` **Short adjectives add -er.** calmer, `[CLICK]` sadder (double the d), `[CLICK]` happier (y becomes i). `[CLICK]` I am calmer today.
10. `[CLICK]` **Long adjectives, and adjectives ending in -ed, use more.** more excited, `[CLICK]` more worried, `[CLICK]` more annoyed. `[CLICK]` She is more worried than me.
11. `[CLICK]` **Irregular.** good, better. `[CLICK]` bad, worse. `[CLICK]` I feel better today.
12. `[CLICK]` **Which is stronger?** annoyed, `[CLICK]` angry, `[CLICK]` furious, who lands so hard that the screen shakes. `[CLICK]` Furious is stronger than angry.
13. `[CLICK]` **Who is more scared?** The class says the sentence. `[CLICK]` Lily is more scared than Sam.
14. `[CLICK]` **Who is more tired?** `[CLICK]` Omar is more tired than Nina.
15. `[CLICK]` **Who is angrier?** `[CLICK]` Lucia is angrier than Jay.
16. `[CLICK]` **On your phone.** Students scan the code or type the link and do the activity. At the end, a few students show their emoji and read their sentence.
17. `[CLICK]` **How do you feel now?** The same question as the start: ask for a comparative. "I feel happier than before!"
