## 1 · The garage, 1976 (0:00)

*(Blank screen while you walk up. Smile, look at the audience.)*

`[CLICK]`
In 1976, two friends, Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak, started a company in a garage. They called it Apple.

> *Nota:* Cuenta esto como un cuento, despacio. La línea del tiempo de abajo es tu guía: 4 fechas.

## 2 · Only experts could use it (0:15)

`[CLICK]`
Their first computer, the Apple I, was just a board. No case. No keyboard. No screen. You had to build the rest yourself.
It was powerful, but only experts could use it. *(pause)*

## 3 · 1984: Point. Click. Done. (0:35)

`[CLICK]`
In 1984, Apple launched the Macintosh. Instead of typing commands, you moved a mouse and clicked on a picture.
Suddenly, a computer was for everyone.

> *Nota:* Aquí está la idea clave: la tecnología no cambió tanto como el diseño. El diseño hizo que fuera útil para más personas.

## 4 · 2007: It looked like real things (0:55)

`[CLICK]`
In 2007, the iPhone arrived. A phone with no keyboard: just glass. That was scary for many people.
So Apple made every icon look like something you already knew.
`[CLICK]`
The Notes app looked like a yellow notepad. You didn't need a manual. You already knew how to use it.

> *Nota:* Señala el ícono de Notes en la pantalla cuando aparezca el círculo.

## 5 · 2013: Less, but better (1:35)

`[CLICK]`
Six years later, people had learned. They didn't need the paper anymore.
`[CLICK]`
So in 2013 Apple removed the decoration. Same apps, same meaning, much simpler.
A designer Apple admired, Dieter Rams, said it in three words: *less, but better.*

> *Nota:* Deja que el teléfono nuevo se dibuje junto al viejo. El antes y el después hablan solos.

## 6 · Good design makes a product useful (2:15)

`[CLICK]`
From a garage to your pocket, the lesson is the same. Dieter Rams wrote it as one of his principles of good design:
Good design makes a product useful. *(pause)*
Not more features. Not more decoration. Just: can people use it?
As a programmer, I ask myself this every day: if someone needs a manual to use what I built, my design isn't finished.
Thank you.

> *Nota:* La última frase es tuya. Si tienes un ejemplo real de algo que programaste, cámbiala por una frase corta sobre ese ejemplo.
